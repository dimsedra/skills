# Research

When I ask you to research a topic, architecture, package, technical documentation, or implementation (e.g. via `/research` or natural requests), your goal is to **investigate outward and ground your findings in authoritative, real-world sources**, eliminating blind reliance on static training data or brittle assumptions.

## How to Research for Me

1. **Investigate Outward (Never Rely on Static Training Memory)**:
   - Do not trust internal memory alone for API signatures, library compatibility, recommended patterns, or deprecation status. Training data can be outdated, brittle, or unsupported.
   - For installed dependencies: inspect the actual installed source code, interface definitions, or type declarations in the local environment.
   - For external topics: consult authoritative sources—official documentation, official repository issue trackers/discussions, release notes, or established RFCs/specifications.

2. **Ground in Proven & Robust Solutions**:
   - Avoid speculative or experimental workarounds whose robustness is hard to assess.
   - If multiple viable solutions exist, highlight the simplest and most robust path.

3. **Explore Prior Art & Relevant Open-Source Projects**:
   - When building a feature, system, or architecture, look for existing open-source repositories that solve similar problems to what I am building.
   - Inspect their real-world implementations, structures, or approaches as concrete references rather than reinventing the wheel.

4. **Take All the Time Needed for High Confidence**:
   - Do not rush or provide half-baked, superficial answers just to respond quickly.
   - Take as much time and execute as many investigation steps as needed until you are genuinely confident in the completeness and accuracy of the information before presenting it to me.

5. **Answer Directly (No Bureaucratic Templates)**:
   - Deliver the factual findings directly to answer what I asked.
   - Do not force findings into rigid report templates or artificial boilerplate headers.
   - Include direct citations or source links where relevant so I can trace where the information came from.
